The journey of specialty coffee in Spain

By the Madrid Specialty Coffee team · 5 min read · 18 Oct 2026

Spain has had cafés since the 18th century. Even so, it took more than two hundred years to ask what its coffee really tasted like. This is the story of that discovery.

The essentials in 20 seconds

  • Madrid's first café opened in 1765. Specialty coffee arrived more than two centuries later.
  • Torrefacto (coffee roasted with sugar) became common with post-war scarcity and still shapes Spanish taste.
  • Madrid and Barcelona led the way. Today it is growing in Valencia, Andalusia and Galicia.
  • It gained momentum after the pandemic.

Eight stops between 1765 and today

  1. 1765

    Madrid's first café

    Madrid's first café is considered to have opened in 1765. Sources disagree on the exact venue, but agree on the date. It was a place for conversation, although hot chocolate remained the favourite drink.

  2. 19th century

    Coffee from overseas

    The coffee drunk in Spain came from the American colonies.

  3. 1936–1939

    The war and torrefacto

    The Spanish Civil War kept imports and consumption low. With coffee scarce, torrefacto gained strength: sugar is added at the end of roasting, the bean keeps longer and defects are hidden.

  4. 2011

    Toma Café, Calle de la Palma

    Patricia Alda and Santiago Rigoni open a tiny place in Malasaña that talks only about specialty coffee. At the time it was pure exoticism.

  5. ~2014

    The scene takes off

    The number of cafés doubles, the first National AeroPress Championship (Spain) is held and the Festival Español del Café (Spanish Coffee Festival), 2015, is announced.

  6. 2016

    A Spanish champion

    Carlos Zavala wins the first national AeroPress title.

  7. 2020–2023

    After the pandemic

    Acceptance speeds up. In 2023 CoffeeFest Spain arrives; its organiser links it to very fast growth in interest.

  8. 2025

    A market that is no longer a niche

    In April 2025 there were 2,215 branded cafés in Spain, chains included. In Madrid, Chamberí is the neighbourhood where specialty cafés have multiplied most.

Torrefacto or specialty: look at the bean

Torrefacto

Sugar is added at the end of roasting. The bean ends up black and shiny, and the taste dark and bitter.

Specialty

Roasted without sugar, designed so the origin comes through.

At the supermarket

Many supermarket blends still contain torrefacto.

Try it

Try your usual coffee and a specialty one, with no milk or sugar, side by side.

Today's figures

2,215 branded cafés in Spain, chains included (April 2025, +3% in a year) Source: Project Café España 2025 (World Coffee Portal), via Perfect Daily Grind
€1,925M specialty coffee market in Spain in 2024 Source: Grand View Research, quoted by El Independiente
60% of the value of coffee in retail shops is capsules (estimate by a Spanish roaster) Source: Perfect Daily Grind (Cafés My Way)
12.5% of consumption is torrefacto (same estimate) Source: Perfect Daily Grind (Cafés My Way)

80% of Spaniards surveyed drink hot coffee every day. (Source: Project Café España 2025, World Coffee Portal.)

Third-party figures. The last two are estimates by a roaster.

Madrid today: from Malasaña to Chamberí

  • Malasaña saw the birth of the first Toma Café in 2011.
  • Chamberí is now the neighbourhood where the most specialty cafés have multiplied.
  • The momentum began in Madrid and Barcelona. Today it is also felt in Valencia, Andalusia and Galicia.
  • A Madrid café owner doubts that supply is growing at the pace of demand.

What now?

  • More education: the industry wants to normalise filter coffee, ideal for appreciating specialty coffees.
  • More chains: McCafé (539 outlets), Starbucks (181) and Santagloria (165) grew by double digits up to April 2025.
  • More price pressure: green coffee is expensive and volatile, and roasters expect the cup to go up.
