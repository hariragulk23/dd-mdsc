What is specialty coffee?

Measured quality, traceable origin and a roast that respects the bean. Everything you need to know, explained without jargon.

Definition

specialty coffee

noun phrase spe·cial·ty cof·fee

Three keys define it. Pick one and check it for yourself.

1 · Score

1.Coffee that scores 80 points or more out of 100 in the professional cupping of the Specialty Coffee Association (SCA).

In addition, defects take points off: a cup with flaws scores lower. Move the slider and see what each score means.

The classic 100-point scale. The SCA has introduced a new method, the CVA, but this scale is still widely used in the coffee trade.

3 · Roast

3.Coffee roasted to show its own flavour, not to hide it.

Not to be confused with torrefacto: coffee roasted with sugar, very common in Spain, that darkens the bean and masks its flavour.

What makes it different

Traceability

You know who grew it, where and how. It is the basis of trust and of a fairer price at origin.

Measured quality

Trained tasters score it with a common protocol. Only from 80 points is it considered specialty.

Freshness

It is roasted in small batches and is best enjoyed in the weeks after roasting.

From farm to cup

Eight stops on a single line. This is how coffee travels before it reaches your table.

  1. 01

    Farm

    Coffee grown at altitude, on farms with their own names.

  2. 02

    Harvest

    Only ripe cherries are picked, often by hand.

  3. 03

    Process

    Washed, honey or natural: the method shapes the flavour.

  4. 04

    Green coffee

    The beans travel in sacks and are cupped before buying.

  5. 05

    Roast

    A tailored profile for each origin, in small batches.

  6. 06

    Grind

    Just before brewing, adjusted to each method.

  7. 07

    Extraction

    Recipe, ratio and time: espresso, V60, Chemex or AeroPress.

  8. 08

    Cup

    Aroma, acidity, sweetness and body. End of the journey.

Tell them apart in 10 seconds

  1. Look at the labelA specific origin and roast date: a sign of specialty.
  2. Look at the beanBlack and shiny: usually torrefacto.
  3. Read the packIf the Spanish pack says "torrefacto", or "café de tueste natural (%) y café torrefacto (%)", it contains sugar-roasted coffee. Spanish law requires those words and the percentages for coffee mixed this way. "Mezcla" (mix) is often used for these mixes, but the percentages are what confirm it.

Glossary

The words of coffee, without jargon

10 terms

  • Origin and process

    Origin

    Country, region and farm where the coffee was grown. The more specific, the better the traceability.

  • Origin and process

    Variety

    The type of coffee plant. Each variety brings a different profile.

  • Origin and process

    Washed

    The pulp is removed before the bean is dried. It gives clean cups and bright acidity.

  • Origin and process

    Natural

    The bean is dried inside the cherry. More body and notes of ripe fruit.

  • Origin and process

    Honey

    The skin is removed but part of the mucilage is left on. Sweetness and balance.

  • Roast and brewing

    Roast

    Heat that transforms the green bean and develops aroma and flavour. Each origin calls for its own profile.

    Try the roast simulator →

  • Roast and brewing

    Ratio

    The relationship between coffee and water. For example, 1:16 means 16 g of water for every gram of coffee.

    Work out your ratio →

  • Roast and brewing

    Extraction

    How much flavour we take out of the ground coffee. Too little: it tastes sour. Too much: it tastes bitter.

    See brewing methods →

  • Quality

    Cupping

    Professional evaluation of coffee with a fixed protocol to score aroma, flavour, acidity and body.

    See the points scale →

  • Quality

    SCA

    Specialty Coffee Association, the association that defines the standards for specialty coffee.
