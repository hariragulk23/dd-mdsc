How much does a cup of specialty coffee really cost?

By the Madrid Specialty Coffee team · 4 min read · 22 Oct 2026

You pay €2.80 for a coffee and someone says it is expensive. But how much of that money is coffee? Less than you think. Do the math yourself.

The essentials in 20 seconds

  • The beans are only part of the price of a cup.
  • Raising the price of coffee by €10/kg adds about 18 cents to an 18 g espresso.
  • The rest of the price pays for the premises, staff, equipment and milk.
  • If you pay more, it should be for origin, freshness and an explanation.

This only counts the beans. It does not include milk, cups, power, water, staff or rent.

Fact

A sector consultancy, Allegra Strategies, estimates that the beans make up less than 10% of the price of a coffee in a café (a figure quoted by La Nación). Your calculation may give more: it depends on the coffee you use and what you charge for the cup.

Source: estimate by Allegra Strategies, quoted by La Nación.

And the rest of the money?

We do not invent percentages. These are the costs every café has:

1

The beans

What you calculated above.

2

Roasting and packing

Roaster, weight loss, bags with a valve, transport.

3

Milk

In a coffee with milk it can weigh as much as the beans.

4

Staff

Training, hours behind the bar, dialling in the machine every morning.

5

Premises

Rent, power, water, licences. In Madrid, the most variable cost.

6

Equipment

Machine, grinders, water filters and their maintenance.

Watch out

Nobody publishes the real breakdown of a Madrid café. Be wary of infographics with exact percentages: they are almost always estimates with no source.

€3 against €2.50

A pioneer of specialty coffee in Madrid described their menu in 2025: a coffee with milk at €3, against €2.50 at a traditional bar.

That is a difference of 50 cents.

In those 50 cents fit: beans with a known origin, a recent roast and someone who can explain it to you.

This is one specific example from September 2025, not an average price for Madrid.

Will the price keep going up?

  • Green coffee is expensive and volatile. Roasters expect the cup to go up.
  • A Spanish roaster raised its prices by €3/kg. On an 18 g espresso, that is about 5 cents.
  • The beans are only part of the price, so a rise in coffee is barely noticeable in each cup.

Source: Perfect Daily Grind.
