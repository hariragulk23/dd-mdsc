Decaf coffee: how it's made, how much caffeine it has and how to choose a good one

By the Madrid Specialty Coffee team · 5 min read · 22 Oct 2026

You love coffee, but not at eleven at night. Decaf has a bad reputation. Often the problem is not the decaf, but the bean that was used.

The essentials in 20 seconds

  • By law, roasted decaf coffee in Spain can have at most 0.1% caffeine.
  • Normal naturally roasted coffee has at least 0.7%.
  • The caffeine is removed from green coffee, before roasting, with water, CO₂ or approved solvents.
  • A good decaf starts with a good bean and a recent roast.

  • Decaf, at most18 mg
18 g

With 18 g of decaf coffee, the cup has at most about 18 mg of caffeine.

Reference figures from EFSA (the European Food Safety Authority). The decaf maximum comes from the legal limit of 0.1% caffeine in roasted coffee; in practice it is usually lower, because not all the caffeine ends up in the cup.

Fact

In Spain, roasted coffee can only be called decaf if it has at most 0.1% caffeine on a dry-matter basis. Normal naturally roasted coffee must have at least 0.7%.

Source: Royal Decree 1676/2012, the coffee quality standard (BOE, the Spanish official gazette).

How is caffeine removed?

It is all done with green coffee, before roasting. Then the bean is dried and roasted like any other. These are the usual methods:

1

With water

Green coffee is soaked and the caffeine is taken out with water and filters, without solvents. This is the one you will see as "water-processed decaf".

2

With CO₂

Carbon dioxide at high pressure carries away the caffeine and leaves almost everything else.

3

With ethyl acetate

A compound that is also found naturally in some fruits. In Colombian coffee it is often made from sugar cane.

4

With dichloromethane

Very effective. It is approved in the European Union with a residue limit of 2 mg per kilo of roasted coffee.

Source: Directive 2009/32/EC on extraction solvents (in Spain, Royal Decree 1101/2011).

Is it safe?

The methods and solvents used in the European Union are regulated and have residue limits.

If you prefer to avoid solvents, look for water-processed or CO₂ decaf, and ask which method was used.

Watch out

This guide is not medical advice. If you have a specific recommendation about caffeine, follow what your doctor tells you.

Five things to look for

  • The method: a good roaster can tell you how it was decaffeinated.
  • The origin: country and region, as with any specialty coffee.
  • The date: a recent roast shows in decaf too.
  • The flavour: specific notes, not just "smooth".
  • The grind: whole bean, or ground when you order.

How to brew it so it tastes good

Use the same recipe as with normal coffee.

If it comes out bitter, try a slightly coarser grind or slightly cooler water.

01Does decaf coffee have caffeine?

Yes, a little. By law, roasted decaf coffee can have at most 0.1% caffeine, against a minimum of 0.7% in normal coffee.

02How much caffeine is in a cup of decaf?

It depends on the coffee you use. With 18 g of decaf coffee, at most about 18 mg. A normal espresso is around 80 mg, according to EFSA.

03How is caffeine removed from coffee?

While the coffee is still green, using water, CO₂ or approved solvents. Then it is dried and roasted.

04What is water-processed decaf?

A method that removes the caffeine with water and filters, without solvents.

05Can I drink decaf at night?

It has very little caffeine. EFSA says that about 100 mg close to bedtime can affect sleep in some people; a cup of decaf is far below that.
