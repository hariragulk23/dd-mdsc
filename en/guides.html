Clean, aromatic cup

V60

The most popular conical dripper. It brings out the best in light-roast coffees, with notes of fruit and flowers.

  1. Place the filter, rinse it with hot water and discard that water.
  2. Add 15 g of medium-fine ground coffee and level the bed.
  3. Pour 30–45 ml of water to wet all the coffee and wait 30–45 seconds.
  4. Pour in a slow spiral up to 240 ml, without pouring water directly onto the paper.
  5. Gently swirl the dripper to level the bed and let it finish between 2:30 and 3:30.

Espresso

Water under pressure through very fine coffee. It is the most demanding method: it needs a machine and a precise grinder.

  2. Add 18 g of fine ground coffee.
  3. Distribute the coffee and tamp it level.
  4. Extract about 36 g into the cup, in 25–32 seconds.
  5. If it comes out too fast, grind finer. If it takes longer, grind coarser.

The pot you already have at home

Moka pot

The moka pot found in almost every kitchen. With specialty coffee and two tricks, it changes completely.

Ratio
1:7 approx.
Recipe
Full basket, not pressed
Grind
Medium-fine
Time
Until it bubbles
Water
Medium-low heat
Body
4 of 5
Cup clarity
2 of 5
Difficulty
2 of 5
  1. Fill the base with already hot water, just below the valve.
  2. Fill the basket with medium-fine ground coffee, without pressing, and level it.
  3. Screw it together using a cloth, because the base is hot, and put it on medium-low heat with the lid open.
  4. Take it off the heat as soon as the coffee starts to bubble.
  5. Cool the base under the tap to stop the extraction and stir before serving.

The moka pot does not need a scale: fill the basket to the top, without pressing.

How does your cup taste?

Choose what you notice and we tell you what to adjust. One change at a time.

Low extractionHigh extraction IntenseMild

Start with how it tastes

Press one of the options. The dot on the map shows where your cup is and the centre is balance.

Under-extracted

The water has not taken enough flavour out of the coffee. That is why sharp acidity dominates.

  • Grind one step finer.
  • Use hotter water, close to 96 °C.
  • Lengthen the contact time a little.

Over-extracted

The water has also taken out the harsh compounds. It leaves the mouth dry and a bitter finish.

  • Grind one step coarser.
  • Shorten the brew time.
  • Lower the water temperature a little.

Too diluted

The extraction may be fine, but there is too little coffee for so much water.

  • Use more coffee: go from 1:17 to 1:15.
  • Check the dose with a scale.

Too concentrated

There is too much coffee for the amount of water. The flavour is overwhelming.

  • Use less coffee or more water: try 1:16 or 1:17.
  • If you like that intensity, add a little hot water to the cup.

Over-extracted and diluted

The grind is so fine that the water takes a long time to pass through, and there is also too little coffee.

  • Grind coarser.
  • Increase the coffee dose.

You nailed it

Sweetness, pleasant acidity and a clean finish. Write down the recipe so you can repeat it.

  • Change only one variable at a time if you want to explore.
  • Try the same coffee with another method.

Water is part of the recipe too

If the water tastes bad, so does the coffee. Pay attention to two things: temperature and minerals.

Temperature

Between 90 and 96 °C for almost every method. Without a thermometer: bring the water to the boil and wait about 30 seconds.

Minerals

Look at the "dry residue" on the label of bottled water. Test how yours does:

Type the number from the label.

Reference: the Specialty Coffee Association recommends about 150 mg/L, within a range of 75 to 250.

How to read a bag of coffee

Everything you need to know is on the label. Tap each number.

Panela, orange, cocoa

  1. Origin and region

    The country and area where it was grown. The more specific, the better: a region or a farm tells you much more than the country alone.

  2. Producer or farm

    Who grew it. If the name is shown, there is traceability: you know where each sack comes from.

  3. Variety

    The type of coffee plant, such as Bourbon, Caturra or Geisha. It affects the flavour, just as the grape does in wine.

  4. Process

    How the bean was separated from the fruit. Washed usually gives cleaner cups; natural, fruitier and sweeter ones.

  5. Altitude

    At higher altitude, the bean ripens more slowly and usually has more acidity and complexity.

  6. Roast date

    The most useful detail on the bag. An expiry date with no roast date is a bad sign.

  7. Tasting notes

    Flavours the coffee brings to mind, such as red berries or chocolate. No flavourings are added: they come from the bean itself.

  • Temperature controlA kettle with temperature control, or a thermometer.
  • Filtered waterA filter jug is enough.
  • Espresso machineAlways with a grinder to match.

Three rules that change the cup

1

Grind just before

Ground coffee loses aroma within minutes. A burr grinder is the best investment.

2

Weigh, do not measure by eye

A scale makes every cup come out the same. It is the cheapest and most effective change.

3

Change one thing at a time

If you adjust grind and dose at the same time, you will not know what worked.
